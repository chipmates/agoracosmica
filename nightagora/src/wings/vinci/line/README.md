# Walkable line

`STUDS` imports all 56 locked collection records and sorts by station then `display_order`. `createLine(materials, selected = 0, language = 'en', phone = false, lettering = 'stacked')` returns a self-contained Three.js `Group` in metres: X right, Y up, negative Z onward, selected stud at the origin. Supply a valid integer selected index from 0 to 55; index validation belongs to the host. `LineMaterials` requires `stone`, `bronze`, `ink` and `dark` materials.

The constructor builds the visible neighborhood of that selection, not all 56 sockets at once: up to three earlier and eight later records on desktop, or one earlier and three later on phone, clipped at the collection ends. The host rebuilds when the selection changes. `STUD_SPACING` is 1.65 m; spacing is deliberately not chronological. `SPACING` and `AFTERLIFE` export the exact collection disclosures. `CERTAINTY` maps the literal data certainty to green (`#71a98b`), amber (`#d7aa58`), coral (`#d17c70`) and visible EN/DE words. Date precision remains in `date_label_en/de`, not inferred from the year cut into the floor.

The caller owns camera, inputs, selection, accessible text and materials. Render the selected `date_label` and bilingual `line` verbatim from the same `STUDS` entry. The drawer carries a plain named-source explanation; the intentionally opened record retains the complete stud, document, holder, qualifications, gaps, source keys and licence. The bench's `bench/visitor-sources.ts` supplies authored EN/DE explanations for all 56 IDs without modifying the collection. A caller must declare the drawer and record roots with the text-register contract; this geometry module creates neither root. Previous/next can reach every entry including reception through 2020.

Each socket slab is nominally 1.59 × 1.63 m and 0.18 m thick, with a 0.256 m diameter opening. A turned bronze rim surrounds a dark recess and the unchanged certainty disc, whose 0.122 m diameter face is below the rim. Dates and certainty words use extruded geometry with only their hidden back caps omitted. The selected year requests 0.38 m cap height in both layouts. Other years request 0.23 m on desktop and 0.27 m on phone; certainty words request 0.087 m and 0.115 m respectively. The host supplies stone detail, reflective bronze and its environment probe.

`lettering: 'row'` is for dates read from far down a floor: each date becomes one row centred on its socket, the year filling 1.08 m along the walk and its word (or word and event as two lines) beside it, every glyph cut long along the walk as road markings are, so it keeps its shape at a grazing eye. Words narrow so a row ends within 2.8 m of its socket. `ROW_LETTERING` holds the measures; `stacked` output is unchanged.

The adjacent 1504 studs carry short event cues: `life-30` has **Anghiari contract / Anghiari-Vertrag**, and `life-31` has **Father’s death / Tod des Vaters**. These authored cues request 0.095 m cap height on desktop and 0.115 m on phone, with a 1.48 m maximum width. They distinguish separate events while preserving the documented/inferred words, colours, dates and disputed-day evidence. The year moves within those two slabs to make space; `STUD_SPACING` remains unchanged.

A 27 m coursed wall bounds the left side near x=-3.65, with a stone base, pilasters and a broad coping: a 0.88 m head over a wider 0.96 m drip course, a bronze bead under the drip and an open joint every 1.6 m, so the parapet has a made head instead of a strip laid on the masonry. Its bronze underside casts the cap's shadow through the same material batch as the handrail. The 21 m rail runs at x=-1.35, y=0.86.

A framed passage at z=-15.56 leaves a 2.4 m opening along the route, and the walk continues through it: paving to z=-23, two coursed returns at x=±1.38 and a cross wall at z=-23.3 with a lit sill. That continuation costs no draw (every part joins an existing material batch) and about 2,100 triangles, and it is what keeps the threshold from reading as a dark panel in a wall. The floor and architecture are generated modern exhibition carriers, not historical reconstructions or a scale model of Leonardo's travels.

`weld(group)` merges static geometry by material and retains `?noweld`. It bakes the meshes' current world transforms, preserves transformed normals, and rebuilds world-projected UVs. Apply it to an unparented, identity-transform assembly before positioning the resulting group. Meshes with `userData.noWeld` and meshes with material arrays are excluded. A merged batch casts shadows if any of its source meshes did, so changing one caster can add shadow passes for the whole material batch. Source manifest identities are retained on the batch.

The constructor does not mutate source data. It returns no disposal helper: the caller disposes returned mesh geometry and any certainty materials marked `userData.owned`; caller-owned materials survive teardown. No listeners or renderer are allocated here.
